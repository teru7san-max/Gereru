# We cling to familiar suffering

> [!quote] Passage (exact)
> People have a hard time letting go of their suffering. Out of a fear of the unknown, they prefer suffering that is familiar.
> — Thich Nhat Hanh, as quoted by the author

## Meaning

Suffering we know has a strange kind of safety: it's predictable, it's part of who we think we are, and we know how to live with it. Letting it go means stepping into something unfamiliar, and the mind often reads unfamiliar as dangerous. So people hold on to an unhappy habit, an old resentment or a draining relationship, not because it feels good but because it feels *known*.

## Core insight

We often keep suffering not because we want it, but because the unknown scares us more.

Монголоор: *Үл мэдэгдэхээс айсандаа бид танил зовлонгоо илүүд үздэг.*

## Critical look

- **This matches common experience** and has a reasonable psychological basis: people tend to prefer the familiar and the status quo, and to weigh possible losses heavily. That is a plausible mechanism, though the quote itself is an observation, not a study.
- **Not the whole story.** Sometimes people stay in suffering because they really don't see a way out, or don't have the resources for one, not just out of fear. "They prefer it" can sound harsher than it should.
- **Provenance.** This line is widely attributed to Thich Nhat Hanh, but the original book or talk hasn't been checked here.
