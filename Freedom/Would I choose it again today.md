# Would I choose it again today?

> ELIMINATE → Харамсал дээр биш, ҮНЭ ЦЭНЭ ДЭЭР СУУРИЛЖ ШИЙДЭХ!
> Мөнгө зарцуулсан ❌ · Цаг орсон ❌ · Ядаж хадгалчихья ❌
>
> KILLER QUESTION: "Хэрвээ би үүнийг аль хэдийн эзэмшээгүй байсан бол, одоо мөнгө төлж авах уу?" ҮГҮЙ БОЛ ЯВУУЛ.
>
> Алдаагаа хүлээн зөвшөөрөхгүй "Би төөрчихлөө" гэж хэлэлгүй тойрч эргэлдсээр хүчээ дэмий үрж хаана ч хүрдэггүй. … WHEN WE ADMIT WE BECOME MORE FREE.

## Meaning

We keep things (objects, projects, commitments, habits) for reasons that point backwards: *I paid for it, I put time into it, I might need it someday, I've always done it this way.* The killer question turns this around and asks about *now*: if I didn't already have this, would I choose it today? The book calls this zero-based thinking. Imagine every commitment starts from zero, and let each one earn its place again.

## Core insight

Decide by value, not by regret: keep only what I would choose again today.

Монголоор: *Харамсал дээр биш, үнэ цэнэ дээр суурилж шийд.* (my own line, #10)

## Critical look

- **Well supported.** The sunk-cost effect (continuing because of what's already spent) and status quo bias (preferring things as they are) are both well documented in decision research. The "would I buy it today?" question is a direct counter to them, and to the endowment effect, where we value something more simply because we own it.
- **The past isn't always irrelevant.** Effort already spent can carry real information: skills built, trust in a relationship, a project that's 90% done. The question is a lens, not a rule. In relationships especially, "zero-based" taken literally is too cold.
- **Admitting "I'm lost" is the hard part.** My note puts it well: circling without admitting the mistake wastes the most energy.

## Connections

- [[We cling to familiar suffering]]: the strongest link in the vault. Status quo bias is one *mechanism* behind clinging to the familiar. Thich Nhat Hanh names the fear; McKeown gives a question that cuts through it.
- [[The spiral shall become a helix]]: "тойрч эргэлдсээр хаана ч хүрдэггүй" (circling and getting nowhere) is the flat spiral. Admitting the mistake is what lets the circle rise.
- `letting-go` ↔ `values`: letting go becomes easier when it's anchored to what I value, not just to what I'm giving up.
