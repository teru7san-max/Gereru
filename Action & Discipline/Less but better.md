# Less but better

> [!quote] From my notes (not checked against the book)
> ALMOST EVERYTHING IS NOISE. (Dieter Rams) … WENIGER ABER BESSER — LESS BUT BETTER.
>
> Instead of making just mm of progress in a million directions you begin to generate big momentum towards things that were truly vital.
>
> A non-essentialist thinks almost everything is essential. An Essentialist thinks almost everything is non-essential.

## Meaning

Most of what asks for our time and energy (invitations, opportunities, tasks, information) isn't bad, it's just not *essential*. Saying yes by default spreads us so thin that we move a millimetre in a million directions. The alternative is to find the vital few, cut the rest even when the rest is good, and put our whole effort where it counts.

## Core insight

Almost everything is noise; my job is to find the vital few and cut the rest, even the good things.

## Critical look

- **Two different kinds of strictness.** The 90% rule ("hell yeah or no") is about *choosing*. "Done is better than perfect" (#40) is about *doing*. Applying 90%-strictness to doing produces perfectionism, and applying it to every choice produces maximizing. Research on maximizers versus satisficers (Barry Schwartz and colleagues) links maximizing with lower satisfaction.
- **Not every choice is optional.** When options are few (a first job, rent, caring for family), a "70" can be the essential choice. The rule fits a surplus of good opportunities.
- **80/20 is a heuristic**, not a measured law. It's useful for asking "which few efforts matter?", not for exact numbers.
- **Cutting good things costs something real.** The book admits this ("eliminating something good can be painful"). Worth keeping that honesty instead of pretending every cut feels light.

## Connections

- [[We want the feeling, not the thing]]: "the undisciplined pursuit of more" (#4) is the behavioural side of chasing things for the feeling they promise. Naval's question (do I want the thing or the state?) is a good filter for what's essential.
- **Lao-Tzu, as quoted in my notes (#39):** "To attain knowledge add things everyday. To attain wisdom subtract things everyday." `simplicity` is subtraction as a way of seeing.
- [[The spiral shall become a helix]]: continuous small edits (#36) instead of rare big purges. Each pass, a little higher.
- `simplicity` ↔ `letting-go` ↔ `lightness`: cutting commitments (outer) often needs releasing attachment (inner) first.
