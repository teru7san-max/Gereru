# We want the feeling, not the thing

> [!quote] Passage (exact)
> The feeling is really what we want in our lives, not the physical things—but the trap is that we believe the physical things will give us those feelings!
> The secret lies within the feeling! All the answers lie deep within your soul!
> — source not yet recorded

The passage continues with a separate idea about hope, which has its own note: [[Hope that it's still out there]].

## Meaning

People often chase an external thing (money, a relationship, recognition, a certain body, a grade, a possession) because of the inner experience they expect it to produce:

| What I chase | What I may actually want |
| --- | --- |
| money | security, freedom |
| a relationship | love, belonging |
| achievement | worthiness, fulfillment |
| recognition | being seen, mattering |

The "trap" is merging the two, so the feeling seems locked behind the thing. The second line turns the search inward: before assuming an acquisition will fix an inner difficulty, look at the desire, fear, belief or need underneath it.

## Core insight

Behind most wants is a feeling I expect the thing to give me, and that feeling is worth examining on its own.

Монголоор: *Бид юмыг өөрийг нь биш, түүний өгнө гэж итгэдэг мэдрэмжийг хүсдэг.*

## Critical look

- **Partly supported.** Research on affective forecasting (Daniel Gilbert, Timothy Wilson and colleagues) suggests people tend to overestimate how much, and how long, a future outcome will change how they feel. That fits the "trap". It doesn't prove the stronger claim that the feeling is *all* we want.
- **Overstated as written.** Some wants aren't mainly about a feeling: wanting a sick parent to recover, or wanting rent paid. Money, health, safety and relationships really do change lives. The useful version is to *notice* the link between the goal and the hoped-for feeling, not to drop the goal.
- **"All the answers lie deep within your soul"** is the author's conviction, not an established fact. Self-examination often clarifies a desire; it doesn't replace information, help from others or action.

## Connections

- **Naval Ravikant (Almanack)**, paraphrased: desire is a self-imposed condition of being unhappy until you get what you want. Naval and this passage share a diagnosis, that the wanting itself shapes the feeling. Naval also argues strongly for building wealth and pursuing goals, so he isn't saying external things don't matter. That tension is the useful part.
- **Joseph Nguyen (Don't Believe Everything You Think)**, paraphrased: feelings come from thinking more than from circumstances. That is close to the claim "the secret lies within the feeling", and has the same limit: circumstances still count.
- [[Words point to the truth, experience reaches it]]: the same move of looking for the answer in a feeling, within.
- Possible links to investigate: `desire` ↔ `happiness` ↔ `attachment` ↔ `freedom`.

## Back to life

> What feeling am I expecting this thing to give me, and is there anything I can understand or change about my relationship with that feeling right now?

This sits *alongside* practical action toward legitimate goals, not in place of it.
