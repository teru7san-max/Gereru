# A journal's value is in review, not volume

> [!quote] From my notes (wording not checked against the book)
> Writing regularly trains you to notice what actually matters instead of letting days blur together.
> Human memory is unreliable — but patterns are durable.
> Writing too much → leads to burnout!
> The value of journal is in review — not volume.
> A few honest lines repeated daily outperform pages written.
>
> 7 хоногт 2–4 цаг бодох, эргэцүүлэх цаг гаргах. (#18)

## Meaning

Memory keeps the feeling of a day but loses its details, so patterns only become visible when they're written down and *looked at again*. Writing a lot doesn't help: long entries burn out and then stop. A few honest lines, written consistently and reviewed regularly, are what turn days into patterns.

## Core insight

A few honest lines, written often and read again, show me patterns that memory can't.

Монголоор: *Тэмдэглэлийн үнэ цэнэ эргэж харахад байдаг, хэмжээнд биш.*

## Critical look

- **This is plausible and matches research on expressive writing and self-monitoring.** Still, the exact phrasing in my notes may not be McKeown's. Check before quoting.
- **Review doesn't happen by itself.** Most journals fail not at writing but at re-reading. Without a fixed time to review, "review, not volume" stays an idea.

## Connections

- **This is the design principle of this vault.** Read → … → **Review** is the step that turns capture into understanding, and it's the step most likely to be skipped.
- **The paper notebook:** short entries (key phrase + one "→" line) instead of full quotes, and a weekly transfer that doubles as a weekly review. It's also why starting simple beats elaborate templates.
- [[The spiral shall become a helix]]: reviewing old notes is re-reading the same page from a higher place. Without review, a journal is a flat spiral.
- `consistency`: Pressfield's daily showing up, applied to reflection.
